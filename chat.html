Quantum Polarization & Malus's Law

In quantum mechanics, when a photon in polarized state |ψ⟩ with angle θA is measured using an analyzer oriented at angle θB, the measurement probability obeys Malus's Law:

P(Detect | θA, θB) = |⟨ψB|ψA⟩|2 = cos2(θB - θA)
Matching Bases (Δθ = 0°)

cos2(0°) = 1.0

Deterministic transmission: 100% agreement between Alice and Bob.
Conjugate Bases (Δθ = 45°)

cos2(45°) = 0.50

Pure random coin toss! Maximally uncertain quantum superposition.
Orthogonal State (Δθ = 90°)

cos2(90°) = 0.0

Orthogonal projection: exactly 0% detection probability in that detector.

Why Eavesdropping on Ciphertext Fails:

Because every quantum key bit Ki ~ Bernoulli(0.5) is purely random and independent, for any plaintext bit Pi and any ciphertext bit Ci: P(Ci = 1 | Pi = 0) = P(Ki = 1) = 1/2 and P(Ci = 1 | Pi = 1) = P(Ki = 0) = 1/2. Hence, P(Ci | Pi) = P(Ci), proving statistical independence.
